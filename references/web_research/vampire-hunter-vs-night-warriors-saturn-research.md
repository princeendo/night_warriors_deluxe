# Vampire Hunter vs. Night Warriors on Sega Saturn

## Japanese and US releases: localization, missing content, and restoration research

**Research date:** November 13, 2026, America/Chicago  
**Scope:** Web-research phase; disc-to-disc verification deferred at the owner's request.  
**Target releases:** Japanese *Vampire Hunter: Darkstalkers’ Revenge*, T-1202G, and US *Night Warriors: Darkstalkers’ Revenge*, T-1208H.

## 1. Principal findings

The regional differences extend beyond the title and character names. The strongest recurring finding is that the Japanese post-ending epilogue text was omitted from the export game, leaving the scrolling character illustration. This is a loss of additional narrative after the main ending, not evidence that the entire ending or illustration was deleted. Sources often describe the arcade releases and Saturn together; their platform labels need careful handling. [3, 4]

A contemporary arcade restoration project provides a particularly useful comparison lead: it reports **14 epilogues, 112 Japanese ending pages, and 323 Japanese victory quotes**, along with changes involving Sasquatch’s victory animations/sounds and Morrigan’s and Donovan’s victory audio. These are the project’s **CPS-2 figures**, not independently established Saturn totals. Its announcement also says that export endings were rewritten and often use different page counts. [5]

The most promising existing official English source is **Darkstalkers Chronicle: The Chaos Tower for PSP**. Two community reference pages describe translated epilogues in its ending gallery; one specifically identifies retranslated endings for Donovan, Hsien-Ko, Huitzil, and Pyron. Those reports justify investigating the PSP release before commissioning new translations, but they do not yet establish a complete line-by-line match to Saturn. [6, 7]

This document is a sourced research inventory, not a claim that every byte, spoken line, or scene in the two Saturn discs has been compared. The distinction matters most for exact quote counts, individual audio triggers, main-ending rewrites, and manual biographies.

## 4. Names and character identity

### Established regional name mapping

| Japanese name | US name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gallon | Jon Talbain | Check abbreviated forms as well as the full name. |
| Zabel Zarock | Lord Raptor | Treat labels and narrative references separately. |
| Aulbath | Rikuo | Search both spellings when collecting evidence. |
| Phobos | Huitzil | Important for robot-group references as well as the playable unit. |
| Lei-Lei | Hsien-Ko | Important for both the fighter and her ending. |

The five mappings are explicitly listed in the regional history entry. [8]

Anita → Amanda is an additional supporting-character change, documented in character references and discussions of later retranslations. Do not silently modernize Amanda to Anita when reproducing the original US script; record that as a deliberate editorial choice. [9]

The Lin-Lin/Mei-Ling naming issue also deserves a check, but the reference consulted explicitly discusses the Japanese name in *Vampire Savior* and its iterations. It should not be presented as a proven on-screen Saturn *Hunter* substitution without locating the actual text. [23]

No complete orthographic audit has been performed for surnames, abbreviations, incidental names, attack names, location names, or transliteration variants. Differences such as spelling, capitalization, and name replacement should be tracked separately from lost characterization.

## 5. Endings: distinguish three different changes

### 5.1 The main ending and the epilogue are different assets

The reported Japanese sequence comprises the character’s main ending, an additional text epilogue over scrolling character artwork, and credits. Export presentation retains the picture scroll but removes the accompanying epilogue text. [4]

A restoration therefore needs to answer two separate questions: does the earlier ending accurately represent the Japanese story, and does the later epilogue exist at all? Restoring the latter does not settle the former.

### 5.2 Character-by-character epilogue inventory

The following is a compact content index drawn from the Japanese transcription collected in the epilogue discussion. It is **not an official English script**. Every row is a target for Saturn capture and PSP alignment; none is marked as individually reproduced on Saturn in this phase. [12]

| Character | Additional narrative to locate |
|---|---|
| Anakaris | His restored ancient kingdom prospers, yet disappears from recorded history. |
| Bishamon | He renounces weapons; the cursed equipment disappears and remains threatening. |
| Demitri | He returns to challenge Belial’s successor, beginning another conflict. |
| Donovan | A Romanian monster’s sorrowful eyes suggest his tragic fate. |
| Felicia | Stardom, catwoman friends, her departed mother, and hopes for romance. |
| Hsien-Ko / Lei-Lei | Reincarnated sisters experience their powers awakening on their sixteenth birthday. |
| Huitzil / Phobos | Centuries later, lifeless Earth prompts the guardians’ departure. |
| Jon Talbain / Gallon | Children welcome him; reconciliation with the village remains unfinished. |
| Lord Raptor / Zabel | His attack on Ozom is compromised by Le Marta’s reporting. |
| Morrigan | Her father’s death makes her the next family head. |
| Pyron | Eternal solitude accompanies his expansion toward cosmic dominion. |
| Rikuo / Aulbath | He discovers an underwater route toward a new homeland. |
| Sasquatch | Overpopulation and hunger undermine the tribe’s ambitions; humans offer food. |
| Victor | Emily stops functioning; Victor waits for lightning to revive her. |

These are narrative units rather than a count of screens. A paragraph may be split across several screens or presented as a continuous scroll. A missing paragraph, missing illustration, changed scene order, and changed display duration should each receive a different record.

### 5.3 Main-ending changes that deserve priority

**Pyron:** The English ending transcript describes Earth’s life force being consumed and the planet burning, while the visual sequence ends with planetary ornaments around his fingers. This makes the route especially useful for testing how much the localization changes the interpretation of shared artwork. The Japanese “collector” comparison is reported separately. [11, 12]

**Huitzil:** The English transcript invokes Tenochtitlan and orders the removal of non-aboriginal populations. These are concrete script anchors to compare, rather than assuming that every English phrase accurately represents the Japanese purpose of the robot army. [11]

**Bishamon:** The English transcript gives him a continuing mission to fight darkness. Capture his main ending and subsequent Japanese epilogue together before deciding how to reconcile a restoration with the existing US wording. [11]

**Donovan and Hsien-Ko:** Include both among the first PSP comparisons because the later-release references specifically identify revised English endings for these routes. [6, 7]

**All remaining routes:** The available research does not provide a reliable Saturn-specific page-by-page change list. Do not infer that these routes have only epilogue changes. Equally, do not label all shared artwork as deleted scenes merely because its text or timing changes.

### 5.4 Ending galleries as comparison aids

The Video Game Museum has a fourteen-character *arcade* ending gallery, accessible from its Donovan page. The pages distinguish Japanese and non-Japanese epilogue presentation. It is a useful visual locator, but its platform is explicitly arcade, and any translated captions must be distinguished from original game text. [18]

A later capture record should retain the source region, platform, character, screen order, complete text, artwork identity, and transition timing. This prevents an attractive-looking reconstructed ending from accidentally combining unrelated versions.

## 6. Victory dialogue and quote selection

The English quote reference identifies low-, medium-, and high-health conditions plus mirror-match quotes. It also provides Japanese quote examples. That is enough to reject the claim that this sequel simply has one English quote for each fighter. The exact number of distinct Saturn strings, the health thresholds, and the priority of mirror-match selection still need direct evidence. [10]

There are two distinct restoration questions:

1. **Inventory:** Which Japanese strings have no equivalent in the US Saturn release?
2. **Selection:** When does the game choose each line, and are any lines stored but unreachable?

A list of translated strings alone cannot answer the second question. Conversely, repeatedly playing a few matches is not a reliable way to establish a complete inventory.

The existing English text should be classified as a semantic match, a loose adaptation, or a different statement. “Different” is not automatically “missing”: sometimes the English release replaces a Japanese thought with a new taunt. Preserve those distinctions so a restoration can offer faithful Japanese content without misrepresenting the original localization.

For later verification, record one entry per quote with the character, Japanese text, US counterpart if any, trigger, source location, and provenance of any proposed English replacement. Deduplicate identical strings separately from shared pointers or repeated displays. Do not calculate an exact “quotes removed” total by subtracting an assumed four-per-character figure from the arcade restoration’s count.

The commonly cited original-*Darkstalkers* quote reduction belongs to a different game. It should remain outside this Saturn sequel’s inventory unless the same behavior is independently demonstrated here. [20]

## 7. Voice samples, poses, and animation

The three Morrigan audio leads are unusually specific: normal throw, command throw, and the victory pose in which she sits on bats. The Saturn-community contributor explicitly describes these from memory, so they should be treated as reproducible test cases rather than measured results. [13]

For each, determine whether the US version uses silence, a different sample, a shorter sample, or a different condition. A sample may still exist in the sound bank even if normal play never calls it. Audio restoration and translation are therefore separate tasks.

Sasquatch needs particular care. A guide explicitly written for the domestic Saturn release documents a hidden steam-from-the-nose victory pose, as well as Morrigan’s alternate outfits and Donovan’s hidden victory poses. The existence of these instructions prevents a blanket claim that all such extra poses were removed from the US Saturn version. It does not establish parity in random selection, trigger behavior, or accompanying sound. [14]

Also separate three categories that are easy to conflate:

| Category | What would establish it |
|---|---|
| Regional omission | Same action and settings on JP and US Saturn produce different content. |
| Saturn conversion compromise | Both Saturn versions differ from the arcade version. |
| Optional presentation setting | Different output follows an Appendix or animation setting. |

The full-animation option is documented for the Japanese release and in a 1996 Saturn review of *Night Warriors*. Match this setting before drawing conclusions from sprite counts. [15, 16]

No numerical inventory of missing Saturn voice clips or animation frames is justified by the evidence collected so far. Nor does this research establish a wholesale English redub.

## 8. Character information, biographies, and manuals

“Character information” needs its own comparison rather than being folded into endings. It can appear in four different places: the game’s on-screen text, the packaged instruction booklet, separately sold guides, and later reference works.

The domestic Saturn guide explicitly attributes its introductory character stories to the instruction manual. Its Huitzil entry presents him as a guardian programmed by ancient Mayans to protect Central America. This is a usable US-side biography anchor, but it does not establish what the Japanese packaged manual says. [14]

The following distinctions remain open:

| Material | Research position |
|---|---|
| In-game biography/profile feature | No evidence in this investigation establishes that a Japanese-only profile menu was removed. |
| Packaged JP versus US manuals | A complete paired scan/transcription has not been compared. |
| Physical statistics | Birth dates, origins, heights, weights, units, and omissions need their own field-by-field audit. |
| Motivations and origins | Compare actual booklet wording; do not substitute modern character-wiki lore. |
| Named relationships | Check sisters, companions, creators, masters, and family names in their original context. |
| Attack names | Keep official localized names distinct from literal translations in fan FAQs. |
| Regional packaging | Cover/back-cover prose and ratings are a separate editorial layer. |

An important search trap is the title **The Vampire Hunter Sega Saturn Manual**. This refers to a two-volume commercial strategy-guide series, not simply the booklet packed with the Japanese game. The volumes are *Ver. 1 Action Handbook for Night Warriors* (ISBN 489366493X) and *Ver. 2 Darkstalkers’ Bible for Super Freaks* (ISBN 4893665057). [17]

Likewise, **Vampire Hunter Perfect Guide** (ISBN 4881992376) is a separately published guide whose coverage includes character profiles and endings. Information in it is valuable supplementary evidence, but its absence from a US game disc is not itself a localization deletion. [22]

## 9. Menus, settings, and other possible differences

Appendix functionality is documented under both regional titles. It includes configurable presentation such as music, scenery, and CPU colors; its existence should not be listed as a Japanese-exclusive bonus. [15, 16]

The following areas remain **open**, rather than confirmed identical or confirmed changed:

| Area | Specific comparison needed |
|---|---|
| Opening and attract sequence | Title graphics, written story/setup, character introductions, and voice cues. |
| Menus and prompts | Mode names, options, confirmations, continue/game-over screens, and error text. |
| Credits | Staff wording, localization credits, logos, and order/timing. |
| Blood and dismemberment | Same attacks and opponents under matched settings. |
| Sexual or comic presentation | Exact poses and artwork; avoid importing censorship claims from other Capcom games. |
| Backgrounds and colors | Default presentation versus selectable Classic/Hunter settings. |
| Music | Track identity, arrangement, playback triggers, and ending timing. |
| Mechanics | Defaults, damage, AI, timing, command recognition, and any regional fixes. |
| Loading and storage | Loading behavior, save identifiers, defaults, and compatibility. |
| Hidden content | Debug features, normally unreachable strings, unused art, and unused sounds. |

This investigation found no adequate source proving that missing characters, stages, or whole gameplay modes distinguish these two Saturn releases. That is an absence of supporting evidence, not a comprehensive negative result.

## 10. Existing English material: provenance and suitability

| Candidate | Best use | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| US Saturn Night Warriors | Authoritative record of the original US localization. | Cannot supply content omitted from that release. |
| English PSP Chaos Tower | First candidate for official English epilogues and later ending translations. | Exact Saturn alignment and complete coverage need verification. [6, 7] |
| English arcade Night Warriors | Comparison source for shared localization. | Saturn wording or logic may differ. |
| Arcade Patches English restoration | Research lead and possible fan-translation reference. | Unofficial, CPS-2-specific, and not proof of official Capcom wording. [5] |
| Fan epilogue transcriptions | Locating passages and understanding broad content. | Conflicting versions and transcription/translation errors. [12, 21] |
| Separately published guides | Supporting terminology, profiles, and contextual information. | Publication content is not automatically on-disc content. [17, 22] |

For a restoration that prioritizes existing official English, the proposed order is: retain matching US Saturn text; check the English PSP gallery for omitted or rewritten material; compare any suitable official arcade text; then explicitly identify the residual material for which no official English counterpart has been verified.

The PSP material is particularly promising, but it must not be described as “all needed official text found” until every relevant passage has been captured and aligned. A later translation can change names or characterization as well as restore omitted material.

### Conflicting fan transcripts

The archived VGFacts thread and the later Reddit transcription are not interchangeable. The former includes a Huitzil passage describing new protection duties and surviving life forms, whereas the Japanese text reproduced in the latter describes zero life readings after centuries. The archived Felicia text also describes a film premiere and charity, unlike the other transcription. These differences are too substantial to treat as stylistic translations. [12, 21]

The Japanese transcription also indicates that Morrigan’s father dies, rather than merely disappearing, and that Talbain must atone for injuries he caused. Those are meaning-level checks for any proposed translation. The online Japanese text itself contains apparent transcription defects, so it remains a locator rather than a verified master script. [12]

Do not assume a translation is official because a forum credits a user on a Capcom-branded community. Preserve the actual chain of provenance: game release, publication, fan translator, or machine translation.

## 11. Disc-comparison handoff

The later phase can start from the register above without repeating the general web search. Its minimum evidence package should contain:

1. **Release identification:** Exact JP/US dumps, hashes, pressing identifiers, and saved settings.
2. **Fourteen complete routes per release:** Main ending, art scroll/epilogue, and credits, captured without skipped transitions.
3. **Text inventory:** Every recoverable string, including alternate or unused entries, with pointer/storage context and observed trigger where known.
4. **Matched audio/pose tests:** Begin with the three Morrigan events, then Donovan and Sasquatch; extend to the whole roster.
5. **Paired manuals:** Actual packaged booklets, with page-level provenance.
6. **PSP reference captures:** Each relevant English gallery ending, clearly labelled by originating game and character.

A useful difference record is:

`ID | category | character | JP location | US location | JP behavior/text | US behavior/text | change type | English donor/provenance | evidence | status`

Use distinct change types for **omitted**, **rewritten**, **renamed**, **reordered**, **retimed**, **conditionally suppressed**, and **asset absent**. An unknown must remain unknown until a capture or data comparison resolves it.

The completion criterion for “all differences” should be closure of this inventory against the identified disc revisions, not merely successful restoration of one ending or discovery of a large collection of Japanese strings.

## Research outcome

The web evidence supports a substantive restoration investigation: epilogue narrative, altered ending scripts, victory dialogue, selected voices/poses, names, and printed character information all require attention. The most useful next source beyond the two Saturn discs is the English PSP ending gallery. Exact Saturn omission counts, complete dialogue alignment, and a definitive list of manual differences remain reserved for the direct-comparison phase.
